About

How might we understand proximity through relationships other than geographic distance? Are there other ways to relate our various encounters with land and place that champion subjective experience without completely abandoning the utility of geography?

DESIRE PATH is an experimental cartographic work that explores proximity beyond geographic distance. Drawing from personal journals, memories, dreams, photographs, and speculative narratives connected to New York City, it locates encounters across four dimensions: place, time, feeling, and knowing. Rather than presenting an objective overview or predetermined destinations, the map is gradually discovered through movement, allowing visitors to navigate a subjective psychogeography one relationship at a time. Built from GIS data but experienced as an interactive multimedia archive and hypertext, DESIRE PATH asks how memory, emotion, and other forms of spatial knowledge might reshape the ways we relate to land and place.
