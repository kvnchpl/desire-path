# Project context

# DESIRE PATH

## Overview

DESIRE PATH is an experimental cartographic artwork that explores alternative forms of proximity between experiences.

Rather than treating geographic space as the sole organizing principle of a map, the project asks whether experiences can also be adjacent through time, feeling, or knowing. The resulting cartography remains geographically situated while simultaneously proposing other ways in which encounters become "near."

The project exists somewhere between GIS, hypertext, digital art, and personal archive. It is not intended to replace conventional maps, but to ask what other kinds of maps might become possible when geographic distance is no longer the only meaningful measure of proximity.

---

# Motivation

Most digital maps begin with an aerial view.

The viewer occupies an impossible position of complete knowledge before any movement has occurred. The landscape is already known. Navigation becomes a matter of selecting destinations.

DESIRE PATH begins from the opposite premise.

The visitor arrives knowing almost nothing.

The map is not presented in its entirety. Instead, it gradually emerges through movement. Understanding follows traversal rather than preceding it.

The project therefore privileges encounter over overview.

---

# Desire Paths

A desire path is a route produced through repeated movement rather than formal planning.

It emerges because people repeatedly choose to walk in a particular way, regardless of how a landscape was originally designed.

The title is therefore both literal and metaphorical.

The visitor follows paths that have been generated through relationships between encounters rather than through a predefined hierarchy.

Likewise, the map itself is not imposed from above but emerges from the relationships contained within the archive.

---

# Encounters

The fundamental unit of the project is an encounter.

An encounter may consist of:

- a memory
- a place
- a conversation
- a photograph
- a dream
- an imagined event
- an anticipated future
- a sound
- a landscape
- a document
- a dataset
- or any other meaningful experience

Each encounter is multimedia.

Rather than limiting an encounter to a single artifact, it may contain any combination of the four supported media types: text, image, audio, and video.

An encounter is not merely an object.

It is something capable of being revisited and related to other encounters.

---

# Nearness

The project proposes that nearness is not singular.

Two encounters may be geographically distant while remaining emotionally adjacent.

Two encounters may occur in the same location but belong to radically different temporal or epistemic worlds.

Every encounter therefore exists simultaneously within several coordinate systems.

Currently these include:

- Place
- Time
- Feeling
- Knowing

Visitors may choose which dimensions participate in determining nearness.

No single dimension is privileged.

---

# Geography

Geography remains an essential component of the project.

Whenever possible, encounters occupy geographic space.

The project does not reject cartography.

Instead, it asks what happens when geographic location becomes one coordinate among several rather than the coordinate from which all others derive.

The physical landscape therefore remains visible even as new forms of adjacency become possible.

---

# Opacity

The project intentionally limits visibility.

Visitors never receive complete knowledge of the archive.

Only encounters that are currently reachable become visible.

This opacity is not intended as obscurity for its own sake.

Rather, it reflects the idea that understanding develops through situated movement rather than detached observation.

The visitor always inhabits one encounter at a time.

---

# QGIS

QGIS plays a foundational role in the project.

Rather than functioning solely as a tool for producing maps, it serves as the analytical environment in which encounters are organized, related, and understood.

The website is therefore one possible interface to the underlying cartography.

The QGIS project remains the authoritative representation of the encounter network.

---

# Future Development

The current implementation is authored by a single individual.

However, the long-term ambition is for DESIRE PATH to become a growing archive of encounters contributed by many participants.

The data model has therefore been designed with future expansion in mind.

Ultimately, the project aims to explore what kinds of collective landscapes emerge when many people contribute encounters that are related not only through geography but also through feeling, memory, and other forms of knowing.

---

# Design Principles

Throughout the project, several principles should remain stable regardless of implementation.

• The map is discovered rather than revealed.

• Movement produces understanding.

• Geography remains visible without becoming absolute.

• Every encounter should be reachable.

• The interface should remain quiet and restrained.

• The project should invite curiosity rather than search.

• The underlying cartography should remain richer than any single traversal.
